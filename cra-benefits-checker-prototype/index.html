Step 1 of 5 — Where you live & filing

Question set A
Are you a resident of Canada for tax purposes?

Most CRA benefits require this. Use a demo answer — not your real status for any official purpose.

Do you file a Canadian tax return every year?

CCB and the Groceries and Essentials Benefit require annual filing — even with no income.

Do children under 18 live with you, and are you mainly responsible for their care?
Are you 19 or older — or do you have a spouse / common-law partner, or are you a parent living with your child?

This is the age / household test used for the Groceries and Essentials Benefit and the Workers Benefit.

Pick a range. This prototype never asks for an exact income or SIN.

Do you have working income of at least $3,000 a year?

Employment or self-employment income. This is the entry test for the Canada Workers Benefit.

Are you a full-time student for more than 13 weeks in the year?
Do you — or a child in your care — have a severe and prolonged impairment, or a Disability Tax Credit certificate (Form T2201)?

How this checker decides

No black box. Each result traces to the “Who qualifies” text in the live sheet:

  • Children at home → Canada Child Benefit; + DTC-eligible child → Child Disability Benefit
  • Resident + 19 / spouse / parent + files taxes → Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (renamed from GST/HST credit, July 2026)
  • Working income $3,000+ & low income → Canada Workers Benefit
  • Severe & prolonged impairment (T2201) → Disability Tax Credit — a tax credit, not a cash payment

Ended — not available

Canada Carbon Rebate is closed. Final payment was issued starting April 22, 2025. It is shown in the directory only as ended — it will never appear in your “may qualify” results.

Not CRA benefits

The Canadian Dental Care Plan and the Canada Disability Benefit are not administered by CRA. This checker does not present them as CRA benefits. Check canada.ca for the department that runs each program.

About this prototype

This is an unofficial concept prototype built for a job portfolio for a candidate applying to CRA taxpayer services roles. It is not affiliated with the Canada Revenue Agency or the Government of Canada, and it is not an official benefits calculator.

How it works

On every load the page fetches a published Google Sheet as CSV, parses it in the browser, and renders the benefits and the checker logic from those rows. If the fetch fails, it falls back to a snapshot of the same rows captured at build time (source checked 2026-11-20) and labels it clearly as a cached snapshot — stale data is never presented as live.

What it demonstrates about the builder

  • Guided rules-based logic: turning “Who qualifies” text into a short, answerable questionnaire with traceable outcomes
  • Plain-language service writing: explaining filing requirements, phase-out thresholds, and the difference between a cash benefit and a non-refundable credit
  • Accuracy discipline: no invented benefits or figures; closed programs shown only as ended; non-CRA programs never labelled as CRA; every benefit linked to its official canada.ca source

Branding note

CRA pages use the Government of Canada signature (no separate CRA wordmark) plus the text name “Canada Revenue Agency,” with FIP red #EB2D37. This prototype follows that pattern as a concept mock-up only.
